Open Chorus

A talking picture board for a child who does not speak

Many hands on one board. Her words never move.

Parents, a therapist and a teacher each change her board from their own phone or laptop, and Chorus keeps every copy in step. It speaks with no signal, and a button she already knows never changes place.

What the circle gets

  • Speaks with no network

    Her board lives on her device and talks from there, in a car park or a lift.

  • New words wait for a pause

    A word added mid-sentence waits behind a small chip until she stops tapping.

  • Both words survive

    Two adults fill one spot offline: one word keeps it, the other waits under “Needs a home”.

  • Try a clash on one device

    A guided demo plays two adults at once, so you can see it without a second phone.

  • See who changed what

    Every change carries a name and a time, and undo takes one tap.

  • Find a word, light it up

    Search from your phone and the button glows on her board, so you can show where it is.

  • Modelled versus said

    Words you showed her and words she said are counted apart, with words of the week.

  • Scanning and touch settings

    Switch scanning, hold time and guarding against repeat taps, set for her hands.

  • Her voice, everywhere

    The voice chosen for her follows the board to every device in the circle.

  • A second language

    The same buttons in the same places can speak a family's other language.

  • Photo to word suggestions

    Snap the fridge and get word ideas for the adults to accept. It never speaks for her.

  • Picture scenes

    A photo of a real place with spots that speak, for words that live somewhere.

  • Paper copy and sitter link

    Print the board for the bath or the beach, or send a sitter a view-only link.

  • Open Board Format, in and out

    Bring a board from another tool, or take hers with you.

  • Private by default

    Counts, not transcripts. What she says stays hers.

Under the hood

Built on Serverpod

Each device keeps its own copy of the board in SQLite. Serverpod's offline sync module carries changes to PostgreSQL and back, and the rest of the server does the quiet jobs around it.
